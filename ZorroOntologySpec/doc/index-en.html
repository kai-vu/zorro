Ontology Specification Draft

Abstract

We developed a domain-agnostic ontology for fault diagnosis in cyber-physical systems that captures the essential concepts and relations required for efficient diagnosis. The ontology was created through analysis of public and proprietary fault datasets, consultations with domain experts from two industrial partners, and a review of existing reference ontologies such as IDO and IOF-MRO. By modeling both operational and functional dependencies, often overlooked in prior work, Zorro enables system-level causal reasoning and better representation of failure propagation.

The namespace for Zorro terms is http://w3id.org/zorro#.

The Zorro ontology is available at http://w3id.org/zorro# .

Introduction back to ToC

Motivation

Cyber-Physical Systems (CPSs) are at the core of critical infrastructure in domains ranging from aviation and manufacturing to energy, healthcare, and transportation. Their failure consequences extend far beyond technical disruption: halting critical infrastructure, endangering lives, and triggering cascading economic and societal impacts. Knowledge-driven approaches leveraging domain expertise encoded in ontologies and KGs have shown promise in fault diagnosis, yet existing ontologies are either too broad or narrowly scoped to a specific domain or process:

  • IDO and IOF-MRO [1], [2]: Provide broad foundational or maintenance concepts, making them too general for granular fault diagnosis.
  • IoT Failure Ontology (IoT-F) [3]: Covers device-level failures specifically in IoT settings.
  • Manufacturing Quality Troubleshooting Ontology (MQTO) [4]: Targets manufacturing quality processes.

Critically, neither models functional dependencies between components, which are essential for reasoning about failure propagation through a system.

Goals and Scope

To address this gap, we introduce the Zorro Ontology, a domain-agnostic, publicly available ontology designed specifically for fault diagnosis in CPSs. It draws inspiration from the part-whole ontology design pattern, as well as from IDO and IOF-MRO, with the following core modeling features:

  1. System & Functional Modeling: Models systems by subsystems, assemblies, and parts where each assembly carries a function via zorro:Assembly, zorro:hasFunction, and zorro:Function.
  2. Failure Propagation: Expresses functional dependencies via zorro:dependsOn, enabling principled reasoning about cascading failures across interconnected assemblies.

Zorro Ontology: Description back to ToC

Zorro is an ontology designed as the foundational framework for fault diagnosis in cyber-physical systems. Its development involved extensive analysis of diverse input sources to distill the most valuable knowledge, with carefully curated concepts and terminology to ensure clarity and consistency throughout. The ontology draws inspiration from IDO and IOF‑MRO: IDO—approved by the ISO TC 184/SC4 Industrial Data committee in July 2023—is notable in industrial standardization, while IOF‑MRO supports semantic interoperability in the maintenance domain through modular ontologies. The figure above illustrates the Zorro ontology’s design. A Component can represent a part, assembly, or subsystem, each performing a specific Function. When a component malfunctions, a Problem arises. Each problem has one or more causes and leads to a specific Effect. To address these issues, two types of Procedure are available: a Solution, which directly resolves the problem, and a Workaround, which mitigates the resulting effects. Both procedure types comprise multiple Steps, with each step involving a specific part of the system
